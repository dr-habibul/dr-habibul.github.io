Biography

I am an Associate Professor in the Department of Mathematics & Computer Science at Stanford University. Prior to joining Stanford, I was a Postdoctoral Researcher at MIT CSAIL. I received my PhD in Applied Mathematics from Caltech under the supervision of Prof. Jane Doe.

My primary research lies at the intersection of Algebraic Coding Theory, Quantum Information Science, and Theoretical Computer Science. I am particularly interested in constructing fault-tolerant quantum error-correcting codes, exploring algebraic-geometric code structures, and designing efficient decoding algorithms for post-quantum cryptography applications.

Research Interests

Algebraic Coding Theory

Construction of algebraic-geometric codes, evaluation codes over finite fields, and list decoding algorithms with applications to modern communication networks.

Quantum Error Correction

Designing fault-tolerant surface codes, subsystem codes, and asymptotic quantum LDPC architectures for scalable fault-tolerant quantum computers.

Post-Quantum Cryptography

Security analysis of code-based public key cryptosystems (McEliece variants), rank metric codes, and lattice-based cryptographic primitives.

Combinatorial Optimization

Graph-theoretic approaches to decoding algorithms, belief propagation bounds, and spectral graph theory applications in coding bounds.
